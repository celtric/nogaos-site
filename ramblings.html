Ramblings

Thoughts out loud I have while working on this project. No AI, no spellcheck, HTML written by hand. Just raw, stupid thoughts, like the good ol' days.

2026-11-18

A long-standing issue I've had over the years is how to model processes. Over time I've settled on a combination of CQRS building blocks and some other stuff I've learned from smart colleagues or come up by myself. But once a process gets complex enough, I inevitably fall in the same situation of: how can I reason about this? How can this scale so a year from now, with all the changes and complexity we'll accumulate, this grows nicely and we keep it readable?

I have some solutions in code, but now that all code is written with AI, imho visual aid is more needed than ever. Multiple people have tried to solve it in different ways (BPMN, etc.), but they all feel way more complex than they need to be (I guess by design; that's the reason why I hate frameworks like Spring: they need to be so generic so to cater for such a wide range of scenarios that they inevitably bloat).

2026-11-17

I had an interesting conversation yesterday with a person that is going to try NoGaOS with some kids. I told her that we should be preparing some "basic competences" guide for kids to learn the basics: folders, files, etc. She said she didn't find that useful, as today "everything is in the cloud". My visceral reaction was "I could not disagree any harder", but at the same time I knew there was a high degree of observational truth in that statement. In part, that's what NoGaOS is fighting against: accepting defeat and giving all our data to large corporations, and losing touch with our own machines.

I've had a lot of thoughts after that interaction, but the main takeaways right now are:

  • This project will continue local-first, using the Internet to augment, not replace. I think they're wrong.
  • The more we go up in the abstraction layer (specially now with AI), the more detached we become with how things work. That builds dependence. NoGaOS is in a great position to fight against that, and give the few kids that want to dig deeper the ability to do so. As such, I'll add a new design principle.

New design principle

We'll try to allow inquisitive minds to tinker and learn how their own computer works. We won't hide behind a "it's in the Cloud, only Google knows how that works" and be satisfied by it. As much as possible, we'll try to give ways for the kids to understand, when they save a file, what's going on at different layers of abstraction. I guess very few kids will take this path, but it's not up to us to decide that, they should be given the chance.
